Designing a user-friendly solution to navigate the complexity of competency assessment management

As a Product Designer at TCS Interactive, I designed the Competency Assessment Tool (CAT) — a role-based web app that helps enterprise L&D teams run consistent, transparent competency assessments instead of juggling manual spreadsheets and third-party tools.

*Blue Harbor is a fictional client name used to protect confidentiality.

Role Product Designer
Company TCS Interactive
Client Blue Harbor*
Focus UI Design · UX Research · Product Design
Live, interactive prototype Built and rapidly iterated on using an AI-assisted coding tool

About

As a Designer at TCS, I was assigned to design a web application — the Competency Assessment Tool — to streamline and optimize the competency assessment process for Blue Harbor. This initiative aims to:

Accurately assess the current skill levels of different functional roles within the organization.

Empower leaders to support individual and team growth.

Challenge

The existing competency assessment process relied on manual steps, making it time-consuming and prone to errors.

This led to inefficiencies, inconsistencies, and difficulty identifying skill gaps.

L&D admins struggled to manage competency frameworks, add or update roles, and keep a consistent, standardized approach across the organization.

Let's learn some terms

A few definitions that come up throughout this case study.

Competency

The combination of knowledge, skills, abilities, and behaviors that contribute to individual and organizational performance.

Competency Group

A collection of related competencies that share a common theme or area of expertise.

Assessment

The process of evaluating or measuring the knowledge, skills, abilities, or other qualities of individuals, groups, or entities.

Defining User Groups

Three roles keep the tool running — think of it as a coaching team.

L&D Admin

The coach and regulating authority of the assessment team. They design the training drills (competency groups), assign players (Assessor and Assessee roles), track everyone's progress, and help them improve their skills.

Assessor

The star player of the assessment game — a skilled referee, carefully evaluating each trainee's performance against the coach's guidelines and providing feedback to help them level up.

Assessee

The trainee stepping onto the field — eager to show off their skills, taking on the challenges the L&D Admin sets, and using Assessor feedback to grow.

Design Process

From the project documents shared by the client, I analyzed the content to devise an effective action plan. To deeply understand user needs, I conducted in-depth interviews across different phases of design, then organized everything I learned into clear groups — giving me a solid, well-scoped foundation to design from.

L&D Admin

Competency Framework Management

As an L&D Admin, I need to efficiently manage competency frameworks for different roles within the organization.
This involves adding new roles, selecting or creating relevant competency groups, customizing competency lists, and assigning appropriate Proficiency Levels.
I should be able to easily search and browse existing competency groups and competencies to avoid duplication.
A visual representation of the competency framework for each role would be helpful, such as a mind map or competency tree.

Assessment Configuration and Scheduling

I need the ability to define and schedule assessment periods for different roles or groups of employees.
This includes setting start and end dates, assigning eligible roles, and specifying the assessment window for participation.
Integration with existing HR systems would be ideal for automatically populating employee data and roles.
Visual calendars or scheduling tools would help me clearly manage and track upcoming assessments.

Assessor Management and Assignment

I need to efficiently assign Assessors to Assessees based on various criteria, such as expertise, role alignment, and potential conflicts of interest.
The system should suggest suitable Assessors based on their competencies and availability.
I should be able to manually override suggestions and adjust assignments as needed.
A clear overview of assigned Assessors for each Assessee would be helpful for tracking and communication purposes.

Assessment Monitoring and Reporting

I need real-time insights into the progress of ongoing assessments, including completion rates and potential delays.
Detailed reports should be available for various aspects, such as individual Assessee performance, competency group mastery levels, and overall assessment trends.
The ability to filter and drill down into reports based on specific criteria would be valuable for deeper analysis.
Visual dashboards with key performance indicators (KPIs) would provide a quick and comprehensive overview of assessment results.

Actionable Insights and Continuous Improvement

The assessment data should be used to identify skill gaps, strengths, and areas for improvement within the organization.
I need tools and reports to generate actionable insights and develop targeted training and development programs.
A continuous feedback loop between assessment data, training initiatives, and competency frameworks would ensure ongoing improvement and alignment.
The system should track the effectiveness of implemented training programs and measure their impact on competency development.

Assessor

Viewing Assessments

As an Assessor, when I log in, I need to see a clear and concise list of all assigned Assessees for upcoming or ongoing assessments.
I need the ability to filter and sort this list by various criteria, such as Assessee name, role, assessment period, and completion status.
For each Assessee, I should be able to quickly access their basic information and relevant assessment details, including the assigned role and competency framework.

Conducting Assessments

Once I select an Assessee, I need a user-friendly interface to review their individual competencies and assign Proficiency Levels.
The interface should clearly display the competency group, competency title, competency description, and available Proficiency Levels with their corresponding attributes.
I need the ability to view detailed information about each attribute and Proficiency Level to guide my assessment decisions.
I should be able to provide optional feedback or comments for each competency, if desired.

Assessment Completion and Review

Once I've completed an assessment for an Assessee, I need a clear way to submit it and confirm completion.
I should be able to review the submitted assessment at any time, including the assigned Proficiency Levels and any provided feedback.
I need the option to download the completed assessment as a formatted PDF for my records or future reference.
A clear overview of assigned Assessors for each Assessee would be helpful for tracking and communication purposes.

Assessee

Self-Assessment Participation

When the assessment period opens, I need a user-friendly interface to complete the self-assessment efficiently.
The interface should clearly present each competency and its corresponding Proficiency Levels with appropriate descriptions and attributes.
I should be able to easily reflect on my strengths and weaknesses and choose the most accurate Proficiency Level for each competency.
Optional space for providing additional comments or self-reflections would be valuable for personal development purposes.

Conducting Assessments

Once I submit the self-assessment, I should receive timely confirmation and access to my results.
The system should present my results in a clear and understandable format, highlighting my strengths, areas for improvement, and overall performance.

Core Pain Points

Lack of transparency

Users struggled to access and understand assessment data, leading to confusion.

Use of third-party tools

The organization relied on multiple third-party tools to manage assessments, forcing users to navigate between platforms and increasing complexity.

Hard to manage multiple user data

Managing multiple users' data was time-consuming and error-prone, resulting in data inconsistencies and inaccuracy.

No indication of performance

Limited visibility into individual and organizational performance metrics made it difficult for stakeholders to assess progress and make informed decisions.

Solution

I started designing the Competency Assessment Tool by focusing on two core principles:

User role-based interfaces

Each user group — L&D Admins, Assessors, or Assessees — sees only the functionality relevant to their role. The sidebar itself changes shape depending on who's signed in.

Simplified field-creation flows

Creating new fields — whether for competency groups, roles, or assessment parameters — is broken into short, guided steps instead of one long form.

Task Flow

The design went through multiple rounds of iteration where features were refined, interfaces adjusted, and information visualization became more user-friendly. Here's a concrete walk-through of the whole system connected end to end, with a small example team.

Scenario

The L&D Admin needs to initiate the assessment for the roles of UX Researcher and Graphic Designer, both of which fall under the Assessee type of role. The Design Lead will be the Assessor for both roles — let's say Ava and John are the Design Leads, with the role already defined in the CAT system.

4 Proficiency levels have already been defined in the system by the L&D Admin:

Foundational Weightage 1 Proficient Weightage 2 Advanced Weightage 3 Outstanding Weightage 4

Creating Competency Group

L&D Admin opens the CAT application, creates the Competency Group, and names it "Design Elements," then selects the four proficiencies that have already been defined.
For the Competency Group "Design Elements," the L&D Admin creates four competencies and assigns the respective proficiencies: User Interview (Foundational, Proficient, Advanced), Quantitative Research (Foundational, Proficient), Color Theory (Advanced, Outstanding), Typography Concepts (Foundational, Proficient, Advanced).

Creating Roles

The L&D Admin now creates the roles of UX Researcher and Graphic Designer, selecting the Assessee role type for both.
For the UX Researcher role, the L&D Admin selects the User Interview and Quantitative Research competencies from "Design Elements."
For the Graphic Designer role, the L&D Admin selects the Color Theory and Typography Concepts competencies from "Design Elements."

Role Mapping

In the Role Mapping section, the L&D Admin maps the Design Lead role to both the UX Researcher and Graphic Designer roles — meaning users with the Design Lead role will serve as assessors for both.

Creating Users

L&D Admin now creates two new users, Anna and Leo. Anna is assigned the role of UX Researcher and Leo is assigned the role of Graphic Designer.

Starting Surveys

Using the Survey function, the L&D Admin initiates the survey for both the UX Researcher and Graphic Designer roles.

Assessments

In the Assessment feature, the L&D Admin assigns Ava as the assessor for Anna and assigns John as the assessor for Leo. Then, the assessment process is started.
Assessors Ava and John receive emails notifying them that they need to conduct assessments for Anna and Leo, respectively.

Assessor

Ava opens the CAT application and navigates to the Assessment tab, where Anna's assessment is listed as pending. Ava selects Anna's profile and initiates the assessment.
Ava selects the actual proficiency levels for each competency: Advanced for User Interview, Foundational for Quantitative Research.
Anna receives a weightage of 3 for User Interview and 1 for Quantitative Research.
Similarly, John completes the assessment of Leo.

Assessee

Anna opens the CAT application and navigates to the Assessment tab, where her self-assessment is listed as pending. Anna starts her self-assessment.
Anna selects the actual proficiency levels for each competency — Proficient for both User Interview and Quantitative Research.
Anna receives a weightage of 2 for both competencies in her self-assessment.
Similarly, Leo completes his self-assessment.

Dashboard

Once the assessments are completed, the L&D Admin views the assessment scores of Anna and Leo on the Dashboard feature, with access to various graphical visuals to analyze the scores.

Inside the tool

A walk through every screen

Five flows, from setting up competencies to reviewing results on the dashboard.

L&D ADMIN · 01

Competency Group

The Competency Group list shows every group with its ID, description and linked competencies, with a single Create button to start a new one.

Creation now lives on one screen: group details on the left, with each competency's attributes built up on the right against the Proficiency Levels chosen for that group — no multi-step wizard to click through.

List view

Create Competency Group

L&D ADMIN · 02

Role

Creating a Role is a single screen too: role details on the left, and every competency from the selected Competency Groups on the right, ready to check off and assign an Expected Level to — no separate steps for details, groups and competencies.

Create Role

L&D ADMIN · 03

Role Mapping

Role Mapping reveals which Assessor roles are responsible for assessing which Assessee roles. A new mapping expands right at the top of the list — pick the Assessee role, then attach one or more Assessor roles, marking any of them as mandatory.

List & create mapping

L&D ADMIN · 04

Assessments

Assessments move through five statuses — Verification Pending, Verified, Assessment Initiated, Completed and Expired. Under Verification Pending, the admin links an Assessor to each Assessee before the pairing can move forward.

Verification pending

ASSESSOR · 05

Assessment Survey

Signed in as an Assessor, the sidebar shrinks to just Dashboard, Assessments and Self Assessments. Opening an assessment brings up the Assessment Survey — every assigned Assessee laid out side by side, grouped by Competency Group, with a dropdown to rate each competency against its expected level and a comment icon for feedback.

Assessment Survey

L&D ADMIN · 06

Dashboard

The Dashboard is the main control hub — a one-stop place for reporting across four tabs: Overview, Competency Analysis, Role Analysis, and Assessee Report. From here, L&D Admins check completion rates, review average scores, spot competency gaps by role, and drill into any individual's strengths and weaknesses.

Overview tab

This case study offers a brief look at the key elements of my work, keeping in mind the limits of screen space — there are many more screens and interactions, such as the Dashboard's other three tabs, that aren't included here.

Final Concept

To prioritize a seamless experience for all user roles, I focused on consistency across the L&D Admin, Assessor, and Assessee interfaces — keeping every screen familiar and minimizing the learning curve, no matter who's signed in.
